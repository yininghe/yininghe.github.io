As a graduate teaching assistant and laboratory instructor in the Department of Chemical Engineering and Materials Science at Michigan State University, I have supervised **more than 80 undergraduate students** across core materials characterization laboratory courses and provided technical guidance and office-hour instruction to **over 100 students**.

My hands-on instruction covered three primary experimental modules, emphasizing measurement principles, safe instrument operation, quantitative error analysis, and physical interpretation of data:

- **Electrochemical characterization:** Trained students to execute and interpret four fundamental electrical perturbation techniques—Chronoamperometry (CA), Chronopotentiometry (CP), Cyclic Voltammetry (CV), and Potentiostatic Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (PEIS)—connecting experimental response curves to interfacial kinetics and mass transport.
- **Mechanical testing:** Guided laboratory sections through tensile testing, hardness measurements, and fracture mechanics experiments to evaluate elastic moduli, yield behavior, and structure-property relationships in engineering alloys and polymers.
- **Structural & microstructural analysis:** Instructed students in Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) imaging and X-Ray Diffraction (XRD) phase and crystallographic analysis, helping them link processing history to microstructural evolution.

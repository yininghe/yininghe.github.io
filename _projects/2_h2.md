## Overview

Hydrogen is a high-energy-density, zero-emission fuel, but its wide flammability range and rapid diffusivity make reliable leak detection essential across production, storage, and transport infrastructure. Electrochemical gas sensors employing room-temperature ionic liquids (ILs) as non-volatile electrolytes offer robust performance, yet choosing the optimal ionic liquid remains non-trivial.

In bulk transport studies, [Bmim][NTf<sub>2</sub>] exhibits higher ionic diffusivity, ionic conductivity, and bulk H<sub>2</sub>/H<sup>+</sup> diffusivity than [Bmpy][NTf<sub>2</sub>]. Paradoxically, experimental sensor measurements demonstrate that [Bmpy][NTf<sub>2</sub>] achieves higher H<sub>2</sub> sensing sensitivity than [Bmim][NTf<sub>2</sub>]. Resolving this discrepancy requires looking beyond bulk electrolyte properties to examine the molecular structure and gas partitioning inside the interfacial electric double layer (EDL) at the platinum electrode.

## Approach

We combined classical non-polarizable molecular dynamics (LAMMPS) and Monte Carlo solvation simulations (GROMACS) to model [Bmpy][NTf<sub>2</sub>] and [Bmim][NTf<sub>2</sub>] electrolytes confined between planar platinum electrodes under operating potentials. To rigorously characterize the interfacial microenvironment where hydrogen oxidation occurs, we evaluated three structural and thermodynamic descriptors along the surface normal (<i>z</i>-axis): molecular number (<i>N</i>) density distributions, cation ring orientation distributions relative to the Pt surface normal, and H<sub>2</sub> solubility and spatial probability density across the interfacial layers and bulk region.

## Key results

- **Two-tier electric double-layer architecture:** Molecular number density profiles near the Pt electrode reveal a complex interfacial structure comprising two distinct double layers: a **first double layer (1st EDL)** marked by a sharp density peak extending to **~6–8 Å** from the Pt surface, followed by a **second double layer (2nd EDL)** characterized by oscillatory wave-like layering extending to **~15–18 Å** before transitioning into the homogeneous bulk liquid.
- **Distinct cation orientation in the inner layer:** Orientation heatmaps and angular distributions in the first double layer near the positive electrode (PE-1L) show that planar **[Bmim]<sup>+</sup> rings lie predominantly parallel** to the Pt surface, forming a tightly packed interfacial barrier. By contrast, non-planar **[Bmpy]<sup>+</sup> cations adopt a much more scattered orientation distribution**.
- **Enhanced interfacial H<sub>2</sub> solubility and double-layer partitioning:** Monte Carlo and MD analyses at 1 bar H<sub>2</sub> demonstrate both a **higher overall H<sub>2</sub> solubility (per ion pair) in [Bmpy][NTf<sub>2</sub>]** and a **higher probability density of H<sub>2</sub> residing inside the [Bmpy][NTf<sub>2</sub>] electric double layer**. This elevated local reactant concentration directly at the Pt reaction plane explains the superior experimental sensing performance of [Bmpy][NTf<sub>2</sub>] over [Bmim][NTf<sub>2</sub>].

## Impact

Working closely with experimental collaborators, this study resolved a long-standing contradiction between bulk transport metrics and device-level sensor sensitivity. By demonstrating that interfacial cation packing and double-layer H<sub>2</sub> enrichment—rather than bulk diffusivity alone—control amperometric sensing response, these findings provide actionable molecular selection criteria for formulating high-sensitivity, high-selectivity ionic-liquid electrolytes for hydrogen safety monitoring.

## Methods & tools

- **Interfacial molecular dynamics:** Classical non-polarizable MD via LAMMPS (electrode/ionic-liquid/gas interfaces under applied potential)
- **Solvation thermodynamics:** Monte Carlo (MC) simulations via GROMACS for gas solubility prediction
- **Interfacial analysis:** Custom Python and MATLAB workflows for number density profiling, molecular orientation vector statistics, and spatial probability density mapping on Linux HPC clusters

## Overview

Additive manufacturing enables rapid, low-waste fabrication of conformal passive components and flexible circuit architectures, provided that structural polymers and functional dielectric inks achieve reliable mechanical integrity and electrical performance. Conducted at the Fraunhofer USA Center Midwest (CMW) at Michigan State University (2017–2019), this project focused on evaluating process windows for Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) 3D printing and formulating high-permittivity barium titanate (BaTiO<sub>3</sub>) / polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) polymer nanocomposite inks for printed flexible electronics.

## Approach

The experimental program combined process-parameter optimization, surface plasma modification, and colloidal ink formulation:

- **FDM process window mapping and plasma surface treatment:** Designed and ran experiments across polymer filaments, correlating print parameters and microwave plasma surface treatments with bulk and interlayer mechanical properties (tensile strength and hardness).
- **Nanocomposite ink formulation:** Formulated BaTiO<sub>3</sub>/PVDF nanocomposite inks by tuning ceramic nanoparticle loading, particle dispersion stability, rheology/viscosity, and thermal curing profiles to improve mechanical properties.
- **Device integration and characterization:** Partnered with electrical engineers to deposit the optimized BaTiO<sub>3</sub>/PVDF nanocomposite inks into additively manufactured interdigitated capacitors on flexible substrates and characterize their electrical performance.

## Key results & impact

- Evaluated how FDM print parameters and microwave plasma surface treatment affect the mechanical strength and hardness of printed polymer structures.
- Formulated and optimized BaTiO<sub>3</sub>/PVDF nanocomposite inks (particle dispersion, viscosity, curing profile) for flexible printed electronics.
- Integrated the nanocomposite inks into additively manufactured interdigitated capacitor prototypes, in partnership with electrical engineers, contributing to the EuMC 2019 publication below.

## Methods & tools

- **Processing & fabrication:** Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) 3D printing, microwave plasma treatment, polymer nanocomposite ink formulation and curing
- **Characterization:** Mechanical testing (tensile, hardness, fracture), Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), X-Ray Diffraction (XRD), Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC), Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA)
